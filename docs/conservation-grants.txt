Here are the best ways to get financial help for your project:

1. Energy Trust of Oregon (Subsidies & Incentives)If your efficiency upgrades involve switching to a system that saves electricity—such as replacing old, high-energy pumps or upgrading to modern, low-pressure sprinklers—the Energy Trust of Oregon provides financial incentives.What they help fund: Variable frequency drive (VFD) pumps, energy-efficient irrigation hardware, and system design.Why it matters: They help lower the upfront cost of equipment while shrinking your ongoing power bills.

2. USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) GrantsThe NRCS manages the Environmental Quality Incentives Program (EQIP), which is one of the largest funding sources for on-farm water conservation.What they help fund: Converting open ditches to private pipelines, installing highly efficient pivot systems, or upgrading to drip irrigation.How it works: They offer cost-share assistance that can cover a significant percentage of the project costs for eligible agricultural landowners.

3. Deschutes River Conservancy (DRC) ProgramsThe Deschutes River Conservancy works hand-in-hand with COID on regional "On-Farm Efficiency" initiatives.What they help fund: Projects that directly reduce your water draw so that conserved water can be legally protected instream to help the Deschutes River.Local funding pools: They frequently coordinate special regional funding pools—such as county-level conservation investments—specifically designated for local irrigation modernization.

They can tell you if your specific property sits along a lateral line scheduled for upcoming piping projects, which often unlocks dedicated funding.Schedule an NRCS Site Visit: Reach out to the local Redmond/Deschutes County NRCS field office. A conservationist will visit your property for free to look at your current setup and help you map out an eligible EQIP grant application.Would you like the direct phone number for the local NRCS field office in Redmond, or help checking if your property size qualifies for commercial agricultural grants?
